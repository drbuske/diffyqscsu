    Handout Daily Prep 0.2 - What are Differential Equations?
    

    Section Overview

    Differential equations are central to science and engineering because the laws of physics are written in this form and they describe how quantities change over time. You have already encountered and even solved simple differential equations in calculus, even if you did not recognize them. This section illustrates what a differential equation looks like and clarifies key ideas like dependent and independent variables. It also demonstrates that many differential equations can have multiple valid solutions, showing how to check a proposed solution by substitution.
    🔗
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1. What Is a Differential Equation?
    Which statement correctly describes a differential equation?
    🔗
    • An equation involving an unknown function and one or more of its derivatives.
    • Correct. A differential equation is an equation that includes an unknown function and its derivatives.
    • An equation involving only algebraic expressions of \(x\text{.}\)
    • No—this would be an ordinary algebraic equation.
    • An equation that always has a unique solution.
    • No. Differential equations may have many solutions or none.
    🔗
    2. Identifying Dependent and Independent Variables.
    In the differential equation \(\frac{dx}{dt} + x = 2\cos t,\) which is the dependent variable?
    🔗
    • \(x\)
    • Correct. \(x\) depends on \(t\text{,}\) so it is the dependent variable.
    • \(t\)
    • No—\(t\) is the independent variable.
    • \(\cos t\)
    • No—this is part of the forcing term.
    🔗
    3. ODE or PDE?
    Is the equation \(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}\) an ODE or a PDE?
    🔗
    • A partial differential equation (PDE)
    • Correct. It involves partial derivatives with respect to different variables (\(t\) and \(x\)).
    • An ordinary differential equation (ODE)
    • No—an ODE has derivatives with respect to a single variable.
    • Neither: it is algebraic.
    • No—it clearly contains derivatives.
    🔗
    4. Determining the Order of a Differential Equation.
    What is the order of the differential equation \(m\,x'' + c\,x' + kx = f(t)\text{?}\)
    🔗
    • Second order
    • Correct. The highest derivative appearing is \(x''\text{.}\)
    • First order
    • No—the equation includes a second derivative.
    • Third order
    • No third derivative appears.
    🔗
    5. System or Single Equation?
    Consider the pair \(y' = x, x' = y.\) How should this be classified?
    🔗
    • A system of ordinary differential equations
    • Correct. There are two equations and two dependent variables (\(x\) and \(y\)), each depending on the same independent variable.
    • A single ordinary differential equation
    • No—it consists of two linked equations.
    • A partial differential equation
    • No—it contains no partial derivatives.
    🔗
    6. Sorting Differential Equations.
      Sort each differential equation into the appropriate category.
      🔗
    • \(\dfrac{dy}{dt} + 3y = e^t\)
    • \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dt^2} - y = 0\)
    • The set \(t^2\dfrac{dy}{dt} + ty = 1\)
    • Linear ordinary differential equation
    • \(\dfrac{dy}{dt} = y^2 - t\)
    • \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dt^2} + \sin(y) = 0\)
    • \(\dfrac{dy}{dt} = \sqrt{y}\)
    • Nonlinear ordinary differential equation
    • \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \dfrac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}\)
    • \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x} + u\,\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 0\)
    • Partial differential equation
    🔗
    7.
    What does the order of a differential equation refer to?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The order of a differential equation is the order of the highest derivative that appears in the equation. For example, if the largest derivative present is \(x''\text{,}\) then the equation is second order; if the highest derivative is \(y^{(4)}\text{,}\) then it is fourth order.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    8.
    Values such as \(x = 2\) are typical solutions to algebraic equations (for example, \(x = 2\) solves \(3x = 6\)). What objects are typical solutions to differential equations?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Differential equations involve an unknown function and its derivatives, so their solutions are functions, not numbers. These functions must satisfy the relationship between derivatives given in the equation. Solving a differential equation means finding a function \(x(t)\) that makes the equation true when substituted.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    9.
    Give a specific example of a differential equation that is autonomous and one that is not autonomous.
    🔗
    Solution.
    An autonomous differential equation is one in which the independent variable (often \(t\)) does not appear explicitly. For example:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dy}{dt} = y(1 - y) \end{equation*}
    is autonomous because the right-hand side depends only on \(y\text{.}\) A non-autonomous example is:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dy}{dt} = t + y \end{equation*}
    because the independent variable \(t\) appears explicitly. Differential equations are classified according to such features.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    10. Newton’s Law of Cooling.
    The time rate of change of the temperature \(T(t)\) of a body is proportional to the difference between \(T\) and the surrounding temperature \(A\text{.}\) That is,
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dT}{dt} = -k (T - A), k > 0. \end{equation*}
    Answer the following:
    🔗
    1. If \(T > A\text{,}\) is \(\frac{dT}{dt}\) positive, negative, or zero? Is the body warming or cooling?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. What can be said if \(T < A\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    Solution.
    (a) If \(T > A\text{,}\) then \((T - A) > 0\text{,}\) so \(\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - A) < 0\text{.}\) Thus the temperature is decreasing: the body is cooling.
    🔗
    (b) If \(T < A\text{,}\) then \((T - A) < 0\text{,}\) so \(\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - A) > 0\text{.}\) The temperature increases: the body is warming.
    🔗
    These ideas reflect the interpretation of differential equations as describing how quantities change.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    11.
    State the order of each differential equation and determine whether it is linear or nonlinear.
    🔗
    1. \(\displaystyle (1-x)y'' - 4xy' + 5y = \cos x\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(\displaystyle x\frac{d^3 y}{dx^3} - \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^4 + y = 0\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. \(\displaystyle \frac{d^2 u}{dr^2} + \frac{du}{dr} + u = \cos(r + u)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. \(\displaystyle (\sin\theta) y''' - (\cos\theta) y' = 2\)
      🔗
      🔗
    Solution.
    Using the definitions, the order is the highest derivative present, and the equation is linear if the dependent variable and its derivatives appear only to the first power and are not composed inside nonlinear functions.
    🔗
    1. Highest derivative is \(y''\) ⇒ second order. All terms are linear in \(y, y', y''\) ⇒ linear.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Highest derivative is \(y'''\) ⇒ third order. The term \((y')^4\) makes it nonlinear.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Highest derivative is \(u''\) ⇒ second order. The term \(\cos(r+u)\) depends nonlinearly on \(u\) ⇒ nonlinear.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Highest derivative is \(y'''\) ⇒ third order. The equation is linear in \(y\) and its derivatives ⇒ linear.
      🔗
      🔗
